About me

I’ve spent my career making complex products easier to understand and use. Today I’m a Senior UX Writer at Amazon, where I work across advertising, conversational AI, creator tools, and Prime Video. Before that, I led content design for Mastercard’s global payment experiences.

I’m usually brought into work that is still taking shape. Sometimes the product has no shared vocabulary yet. Sometimes the team needs to untangle a flow, explain an unfamiliar capability, or make a high-stakes decision understandable without oversimplifying it. I help define the experience and stay with it through writing, research, implementation, and launch.

At Mastercard, I also managed and mentored a content designer, established an unmoderated research practice, led workshops and critiques, and presented recommendations to senior leaders.

I’m a hands-on writer. I work on content models, taxonomy, information architecture, and flows, then write and refine the interface. The structure matters, and so does whether each string is clear, useful, and right for the moment.

I work closely with product, design, research, engineering, science, legal, and privacy while the direction is still flexible. That is when content design can do the most useful work: exposing assumptions, clarifying tradeoffs, and helping the team make better decisions.

How I work

Four habits

  1. Understand the product and the decision

    Before I write, I make sure I understand what the product does, what someone needs to decide, and what information they need to decide it.

  2. Write for the actual context

    I design for the context in which someone will use the product. That might mean navigating with a remote control, watching a live game, building an ad campaign, or trying to complete a payment.

  3. Bring the right people in early

    I involve product, design, research, engineering, science, legal, and privacy before the work hardens. It is easier to resolve a problem while the team still has room to change the product.

  4. Test the work and document what we learn

    I use research and experiment data to challenge assumptions, then document successful decisions so other teams can reuse them.
